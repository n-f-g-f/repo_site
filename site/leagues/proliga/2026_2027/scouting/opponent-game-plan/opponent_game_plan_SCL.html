How They Play

Tempo
Below-average pace; they are more comfortable when the game stays organized in the half court.
Ball Movement
Above-average assist profile; expect cleaner passing sequences and more extra-pass possessions.
Offensive Glass
Second-chance threat; the first stop is not enough against this team.
Turnovers
Better-than-average ball security; they do not hand away many live possessions.
Physicality
Lower foul volume than league average; this is not a team that wants constant contact chaos.
Shot Mix
Perimeter volume is close to league average; shot mix alone does not define them.

Game Plan in 4 Keys

  • Control the rhythm: they are more comfortable in a controlled half-court game, so do not let them play cleanly into structure.
  • Finish possessions: the first stop is not enough against this team because the offensive glass is a real source of extra points.
  • Load into S. Evans: he is the main organizer, so make secondary creators finish possessions.
  • Closeout discipline matters: S. Evans, B. Almeida are the first perimeter names you cannot lose in help situations.

Scouting Notes

Scouting Notes

Offense

  • The main offensive references are S. Silva and J. Neves; load the scouting plan into those names first.

Perimeter Shooting

  • Priority closeouts start with S. Evans, B. Almeida, and S. Silva; do not lose them in help-and-recover situations.
  • Some perimeter volume comes from B. Almeida and S. Silva; contest, but do not over-help into secondary advantages.

Scoring Areas

  • Their offense naturally flows toward Above the Break 3; protect that area early in possessions.
  • Several players have clear comfort zones, especially S. Evans, S. Silva, and V. Markovetskyy; force them away from preferred areas.

Units

  • Recognize the main duo/trio combinations: J. Neves | S. Silva and J. Neves | S. Silva.

Defensive Emphasis

  • Second-chance threat; the first stop is not enough against this team.
  • Do not lose S. Evans and B. Almeida in help-and-recover situations.
